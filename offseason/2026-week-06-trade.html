Hot Take · Week 6 SnSAllStars

aPukalypse Now just won the title. In October.

Let me make this ABUNDANTLY clear. Half an hour, THIRTY MINUTES, after this very page called aPukalypse Now's quarterbacks the worst in SnS, they went out and got LAMAR JACKSON. And Emergency Room, sitting there at 0–5, zero and five, handed him over for Jaxson Dart, Breece Hall, Braelon Allen, Roman Wilson and a second-round pick. Now, the spreadsheet people are going to tell you Emergency Room won this trade. The spreadsheet people need to put the calculator DOWN.

Here are the facts. Four players each way, and a pick each way. aPukalypse Now gets 16,406 in KTC value, average age 29.4, plus Emergency Room's 2027 sixth, which projects to 6.01 and which KTC will not even put a NUMBER on. A sixth-round pick! That's not an asset, that's a parking ticket. Emergency Room gets 17,145, average age 24.2, plus aPukalypse's 2027 second, projected 2.11 and worth about 3,213. So yes, on paper, Emergency Room comes out ahead: 739 in players, about 3,950 with the pick. HOWEVER. The single most valuable piece in this entire deal is Lamar Jackson, at 7,275, and he is not going to Emergency Room. He is going to a team that is 4–1.

Worst to third. In ONE trade. Earlier today, and I want everybody to remember this, aPukalypse Now had the worst quarterback room in the league, QB12, and they were 4–1 ANYWAY, winning on the best receivers in SnS. Now they're QB3. They went from seventh on present strength to second, and the only team ahead of them is G.G.G. That's not a trade, that's a WARNING. Meanwhile Emergency Room, zero and five, just took its quarterbacks from QB2 to QB8, its receivers to dead last, and its present strength from tenth to eleventh. The one bright spot? The backfield goes from RB11 to RB10. Pop the champagne.

Now here's where the spreadsheet people LOSE THEIR MINDS. Emergency Room took in more value, with players five years younger on average, and its keeper capital went DOWN. Down! 521 points, gone, because Lamar, Saquon and DJ Moore were three of its best keepers, and Eli Stowers falls out of the top fourteen to make room for the new guys. And aPukalypse Now? Its keeper capital went UP, 768, because Lamar, Saquon and Moore slide straight into the spots Dart, Hall and Allen left behind. Keeper capital counts your best fourteen players and no picks. So what you are looking at is one team that swapped three keepers for three better ones, and another that swapped three great ones for four maybes and a second-round lottery ticket.

Where it moves them

The contention cycle from the Week 6 update, redrawn after the trade. Hollow rings show where aPukalypse Now and Emergency Room were standing before it; arrows show where they ran off to. The other ten teams didn't do ANYTHING, so they're faded out.

Look at the chart. LOOK at it. aPukalypse Now went sprinting to the right, from just past the middle on present strength to second-best, and even ticked UP on dynasty value. Emergency Room went sliding to the left, deeper into the rebuild, and ticked DOWN. Neither team changed corners. One of them is in the corner where you win championships. The other one is in the corner where you get the first pick.

Who won

I'm going to say it, and I don't care who doesn't like it: aPukalypse Now just won the championship. In October. They're 4–1, second in the table, second on present strength, and they did it without lowering their keeper capital by a single point. It went UP. Yes, Lamar, Saquon and DJ Moore are all 29. I hear you. I HEAR you. But nobody hangs a banner for average age. And Emergency Room? Look, I respect a rebuild. Four players aged 22 to 25, the higher pick, more KTC on paper. Lovely. Beautiful. Put it in a museum. You are 0–5, and you just traded the most valuable player in the deal for a rebuild you could have started in August. That's my take, and I am STICKING to it.
