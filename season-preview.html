2026 Season Preview SnSAllStars

Four teams have a plan. Eight have a roster.

Fourteen keepers is enough rope to hang yourself with, and this year most of the league used it to tie a knot in the middle. Of twelve rosters, exactly two are honestly built to win in 2026 and two are honestly built for later. The other eight are sitting on a pile of players nobody would describe as a direction — good enough to make the playoffs, not good enough to matter, too valuable to tear down. Here is how all twelve end up.

The league in two questions

Can you win this year, and will you still be able to win in three? Those are different questions, and a keeper-14 league punishes you for confusing them. Plotted below: every roster's win-now strength against its dynasty strength, coloured by which way it leans.

01

Buckeye Air Raid

Contender Deepest WR room Best QB duo

Every league has one team that gets good and then gets lucky. This is that team, and it is not close.

Burrow and Hurts is the only superflex room in SnS that does not require a weekly act of faith. A QB2 worth 6,033 does not sound like a headline until you learn the league median is around 4,500 and the bottom of the table is starting Michael Penix Jr. Two starting slots, two quarterbacks you would happily start anywhere. Nobody else can say that.

Then there is 34,613 in receiver capital, the most in the league, and it is not top-heavy the way Chase or Puka make a room top-heavy. London, Flowers, Waddle, Harrison, Addison, Burden — six names, every one of them a plausible WR2, which means bye weeks cost nothing and there is a trade chip at every price point from October onward.

The honest knock: the backfield is a rookie and Omarion Hampton, and Harold Fannin Jr. is being asked to be the starting tight end on a title team. Both are fixable in November with the receiver surplus. Everyone else's problems are structural.

VerdictWins the $1,200 unless the injury report turns personal. The scary part is that the roster is young enough to do it again in 2028.

02

G.G.G

Contender Most capital No spine

The best collection of players in the league, assembled by someone who forgot which format they were in.

Top of the power rankings — though in SnS that just means top of the keeper-capital column, since the ranking is that column sorted and reprinted. The capital is real, though: Gibbs, Jefferson, McBride, McConkey, Garrett Wilson, DeVonta Smith. Elite at running back, elite at tight end, four receivers you would start on any roster here.

Then the lineup asks for a second quarterback and Sam Darnold is standing there with his hands out. Twelve thousand and fifty-seven in QB capital — third-lowest in a league that starts two of them — split between a quarterback who has been about to break out for three consecutive seasons and one whose career is a Rorschach test. Every other team at the top of this page starts two quarterbacks they are happy about. This one starts one it is hopeful about and one it has to explain.

The good news is that the fix is affordable: nobody in SnS has more spare receiver value to spend, and there are four teams below who would take a DeVonta Smith call.

VerdictLeads the league in points, finishes third in wins, loses a semi-final by nine, and spends the offseason insisting it was the best team. It was.

aPukalypse Now

Contender WR heavy QB agnostic

Named the team after Puka Nacua and has been obligated to keep being right about it ever since. So far, so good.

Puka, Nabers, A.J. Brown and Tee Higgins is 30,924 in receiver capital and four genuine every-week starters, and Tyler Warren is a real tight end at a position where half this league is starting a committee. On raw weekly ceiling this is a top-three roster and the age curve mostly cooperates — Nabers and Dart are 23, Puka and Warren are 25.

The tax comes due at quarterback. Trevor Lawrence and Jaxson Dart, with Ty Simpson stashed behind them as a third lottery ticket: three quarterbacks, 15,776 in capital, and not one you would want starting a playoff game. This is a roster built by somebody who understands receivers at a deep level and treats quarterback as a rounding error — a defensible worldview in every format except the one they are playing.

VerdictMakes the money. Does not win it. Trades for a real quarterback in 2027 and immediately becomes the problem.

The Jackson 10

Tilted to now Most QB capital Oldest skill group

The most quarterback capital in the league, in a league with two quarterback slots.

Eighteen thousand and twenty-five across Lamar, Mahomes and Baker Mayfield. Two of them start. The third — 4,559 of value — sits on the bench every Sunday as the most expensive handcuff in the sport. It is genuinely a great problem to have and it is still, unmistakably, a problem: that is a starting receiver's worth of value doing nothing.

Behind the quarterbacks the roster reads like a reunion. Saquon, Mahomes, DJ Moore, Courtland Sutton, Chris Godwin, Dallas Goedert — all of them past the point where dynasty value goes up. The best receiver on the roster is Emeka Egbuka, who is also the only young one worth naming. Rookie capital is 4.8%, eleventh of twelve.

Lamar and Achane will win a lot of weeks in 2026. The 2028 version of this roster is a rebuild that has not been informed yet.

VerdictGenuinely dangerous in a one-week playoff format. Push every chip in before the December 9 deadline, because there will be nothing to sell in the spring.

05

Gronkamole and Chips

Tilted to now Zero rookies All in

Zero. Not low — zero. The only roster in SnS without a single rookie among its fourteen keepers.

Every other team in the league kept at least one. This one looked at the 2026 class and declined, entirely, on principle. What it kept instead is Christian McCaffrey, Derrick Henry, Matthew Stafford and 22,918 in running back capital — second-most in a league where running backs depreciate faster than anything else on the board.

And it might work. Jaxon Smith-Njigba at 9,192 is the second-most valuable receiver in SnS. Tucker Kraft is a genuine tight end. Bucky Irving and TreVeyon Henderson are the young legs holding the whole structure up. A healthy McCaffrey and a healthy Henry is a top-three scoring team, and nobody in this league can match the floor of that backfield in November.

But there is no plan B. No young core to pivot to, no stashed rookies, nothing to sell in a bad October. Malik Willis is the tenth-most valuable keeper on the roster, which tells you how thin the tail is. If this does not cash in 2026, the rebuild starts below sea level.

VerdictTitle or ruin, and the roster knows it. Respect for the commitment; the exit is going to be brutal.

The Goonies

Playoff team Most top-heavy Worst TE room

Ja'Marr Chase and Drake Maye is 18,890 of value in two players. Everything after that is a negotiation.

The top three keepers are 37% of the roster, the most concentrated build in the league. When Chase and Maye go off, this team beats anyone on the page — that is a legitimate championship path in a format where one week decides everything.

The trouble starts when the lineup card asks for a second receiver and the answer is Christian Watson or Alec Pierce, then asks for a tight end and the answer is Kyle Pitts. Still. In 2026. At 4,320, the worst tight end room in SnS by capital. Makai Lemon and Ja'Kobi Lane are real future pieces who will not help in October, and Daniel Jones is a kept player on a contending roster, a sentence I have now read back to myself four times.

Two acquisitions from serious, and 15.5% in rookie capital plus a genuine WR1 means it has the currency to go get them.

VerdictMakes the playoffs on Chase's back, loses a round to a deeper team, then wins the offseason.

Dirty Shorts

Tilted to the future Best young core Worst QB2

The best young roster nobody is worried about yet, currently starting Michael Penix Jr. in the superflex.

Start with the good, because it is very good: Jeanty, Loveland, Brian Thomas Jr., Carnell Tate, Nico Collins, Chase Brown. In three years this is the best roster in SnS and it is not especially close. Everything here is young, ascending, and cheap to hold.

Now the problem. A QB2 worth 2,759 — dead last in a twelve-team league that starts two quarterbacks every single week. The next-worst is Bryce Young at 3,714, and the league's best is more than double it. That is not a soft spot, it is a hole in the lineup card that opens every Sunday.

Meanwhile 24,878 sits in running backs, the most in the league, spread across Jeanty, Chase Brown, Corum, Dowdle, Chris Rodriguez and Tank Bigsby. Six running backs and one quarterback, in a format that inverts exactly that priority. Ship three of those backs for a quarterback nobody wants to say out loud and this is a top-four team by Halloween.

VerdictPlayoff bubble this year, everybody else's problem for the next four. The only thing standing between it and the top of this page is one phone call.

08

Allen or Nothing

Bubble Single point of failure

The team name is a risk disclosure and it should be read as one.

Josh Allen is 9,960 of a 60,631 roster. Sixteen percent of everything, in one player, the highest single-player concentration in SnS — and the top three keepers are 37% of the team. There is a version of this season where Allen finishes as the overall QB1 and this roster wins ten games, and there is a version where he misses three weeks in November and the whole thing is on the wire by Thanksgiving.

Behind him it thins quickly and ages faster. Mike Evans, Mark Andrews, Aaron Jones and Dak Prescott are all on the wrong side of the curve, and the tight end room is 4,871 — second-worst in the league. Jonathan Taylor and Tetairoa McMillan are genuinely good, and Jordyn Tyson is the sharpest rookie bet on any roster here.

Verdict7–7, in the hunt until Week 13, one hamstring away from a fire sale. The name was a warning label the whole time.

LoUdPaXx President

Missing the playoffs Least QB capital

Brock Bowers is the most valuable tight end in the league. Enjoy him, because the rest of this is difficult.

At 8,264 Bowers is a genuine weekly advantage at the position where most of SnS is guessing, and he is 24. That is the good part and it is a real one.

Now: nine thousand, three hundred and fifty-two. That is the total quarterback capital on a roster required to start two of them every week, and it is the lowest figure in the league. It buys Brock Purdy and Bryce Young. In a superflex format that is not a disadvantage, it is a structural weekly deficit of maybe fifteen points before the rest of the lineup gets a vote.

Around them, Davante Adams and DK Metcalf are aging out, the backfield is Javonte Williams and Cam Skattebo doing their honest best, and Travis Hunter has fallen to 2,938 — once a can't-miss asset, now the twelfth-best keeper on a ninth-place roster.

VerdictBowers wins four games by himself. That is the number. Four.

OutForTheSeason

Two stars Steepest cliff Worst TE room

Bijan Robinson and CeeDee Lamb are 17,173 of unarguable production. The other twelve keepers are a rumour.

Bijan at 9,999 is tied for the most valuable asset in the league and CeeDee is a genuine WR1. Then comes the steepest drop in SnS: from Lamb at 7,174 to the third-best keeper, Tyler Shough, at 4,780 — a valuation I have now checked against the source data three times and still refuse to accept.

The quarterback plan is Shough, C.J. Stroud and Kyler Murray: three men, 13,090 in combined capital, and a combined effect on your Sunday best described as a low hum. The tight end room is George Kittle and nothing at all, 3,312, the lowest in the league. Kittle and Terry McLaurin are also the two oldest useful players on the roster, which is not where you want your remaining reliability concentrated.

The team name has been functioning as a disclaimer for some time now and nobody has asked it to stop.

VerdictBijan carries, CeeDee helps, everything else abstains. 5–9 with two absolutely enormous weeks.

Bo Knows Football

Flattest roster Three tight ends

Amon-Ra St. Brown, and then a long, level plain of adequacy stretching to the horizon.

Nine of the fourteen keepers sit between 2,600 and 3,800 — the flattest roster in the league. No star to build around beyond St. Brown, and no true dead weight to cut either, which sounds like balance and is actually the worst place to be in a keeper-14 format: nothing worth trading for, nothing worth trading away.

The tight end situation deserves its own paragraph. Dalton Kincaid, Jake Ferguson and AJ Barner — 8,909 in capital across three players for one lineup slot, and not one of them will ever be the reason a week is won. That is fourth-most TE capital in SnS producing roughly eleventh-best TE production.

There are real things here. Quinshon Judkins and Jadarian Price is a genuinely interesting young backfield, Matthew Golden is 23 and ascending, and Bo Nix and Jared Goff will quietly clear the superflex bar every week without ever winning one. Nothing is broken. Nothing is good.

Verdict6–8, tenth in points, no notes, no memories, no reason to check the app after Week 10.

Extended Warranty

Tilted to the future Most rookie capital Honest

Last in capital, last in the power ranking, and the only team on this page executing a coherent plan.

A full quarter of the roster — 13,546 of 53,257 — is tied up in players who will score approximately nothing in 2026: Fernando Mendoza, Malachi Fields, Carson Beck, Emmett Johnson. That is 25.4% in rookie capital, ten points clear of second place. Jayden Daniels and George Pickens are the only two assets here that are simultaneously good and available this season.

None of that is a criticism. In a fourteen-keeper league where eight teams are stuck in the middle owning nothing anybody wants, being genuinely bad on purpose is the sharpest position available. The team is named Extended Warranty and it is, in fact, paying now for coverage later.

The concern is not the strategy, it is the shopping list. Rebuilds are won by acquiring the right young players, not merely young ones, and Mendoza at 5,237 is the third-most valuable quarterback in SnS that nobody will start this year. If he hits, this is a 2028 contender. If he does not, this is just a bad team that took the scenic route.

Verdict3–11, and the least embarrassing 3–11 you will ever watch. Ask again in two years.
